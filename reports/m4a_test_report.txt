   guarantee): SUM(attributed_downloads) GROUP BY attribution_model must be
   identical across 'first_touch' and 'last_touch', because the same
   linkable population (6,863 rows minus non-completers/non-linkable, see
   silver.linked_journeys) is counted twice. Verified in
   tests/test_medallion.py::TestAttributionFirstVsLast — both models total
   6,863 rows' worth of downloaders and additionally match
   COUNT(*) FROM silver.linked_journeys exactly.

b. Cross-tab completeness: gold.completion_by_channel_device's row count
   equals COUNT(*) of the distinct (channel, device_category) pairs
   actually present — no duplicate or missing grid cells. All
   completion_rate values verified in [0, 1] and users_completed <=
   users_started for every row (tests/test_medallion.py::
   TestCompletionByChannelDevice).

c. Planted H1/H2 confound, read off the mart (no hard-coded literals):

   df["mobile"] pulled live from the mart, holding for every channel in
   the table above, not just paid_social_meta.


4. THE DIFFERENT-STORIES FINDING
------------------------------------
Do first-touch and last-touch actually tell different stories in this
data? NO — in this synthetic dataset they are IDENTICAL, channel by
channel, not just in grand total: every one of the 6,863
silver.linked_journeys rows has acquisition_channel exactly equal to its
last-touch-mapped channel (0 mismatches, checked directly against the
silver layer). The biggest mover between the two models is 0 downloads /
0.0 percentage points, for every channel — there is no channel whose
share shifts at all.

This is a property of how the data was generated (src/generate_data.py
assigns each synthetic user exactly one channel for the whole
session-generation process; nothing in the generator models a user
switching campaigns between their first and their most recent web
session), not evidence that first-touch/last-touch attribution is
generally redundant — docs/knowledge/attribution.md's example (a
paid-acquired user returning organically) describes a real phenomenon
this particular dataset simply never instantiates. The mart and KPI are
still correct and still worth shipping: they report what the data
actually shows (perfect agreement here) rather than assuming disagreement
that isn't present, and they will correctly surface a split the moment a
real dataset has one.


5. DEVIATIONS FROM THE TASK'S LITERAL WORDING
------------------------------------------------
- completion_by_channel_device's "channel" column is the utm_campaign ->
  channel NAME mapping given in the task (summer_hearing_meta ->
  paid_social_meta, etc.), not the raw utm_campaign string that the
  pre-existing gold.completion_by_channel mart uses. This was chosen so
  "channel" means the same thing (the same 5-value vocabulary) in both new
  M4a marts and is directly comparable to gold.attribution_first_vs_last's
  channel column; the pre-existing gold.completion_by_channel mart is left
  untouched (still keyed by raw last_touch_campaign) since changing it was
  out of scope and would have broken its KPI-equivalence oracle test.
- Beyond the 7 numbered build steps, three small pre-existing "10 KPI"
  mentions were corrected to 12 for consistency, since they would
  otherwise be immediately, visibly wrong once this mart lands:
  docs/api_contract.md's /api/metrics and /api/dashboard descriptions, and
  app/static/index.html's "Build top-10 KPI dashboard" button label
  (EN+TR). No test depended on the old "10"/"top-10" wording; this is a
  documentation/label correction only, not a behavior change.
- Per the task's "keep the strongest four" instruction, the two chips
  removed were "Which channel completes the hearing test best?" (now
  subsumed by the new, more informative channel x device cross-tab chip)
  and "Weekly trend of test starts" (a plain trend, least connected to the
  hypothesis-testing theme the other five chips share).
